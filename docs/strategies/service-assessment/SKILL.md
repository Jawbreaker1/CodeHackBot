# Service and protocol assessment

1. Bind each observation to a target address, port or endpoint, transport, and time. Distinguish a banner, probe inference, and authenticated version evidence; avoid treating product names from one scanner as fact.
2. Identify the service's role and trust boundary. Compare accessible functionality, authentication requirements, and configuration with the expected deployment. Follow surprising behavior with a narrow independent check.
3. Research product-specific leads only after identity and prerequisites are credible. Separate an exposed service, a vulnerable version hypothesis, and a reproduced weakness.
4. Allocate workers by distinct services or protocols when useful. Share concise observations and evidence references, not entire scan logs; coordinate any state-changing checks.
5. Report exact probe settings, responses, confidence, and coverage gaps. A filtered or silent endpoint is a bounded negative observation.

Use installed protocol clients and Kali tools after verifying capabilities and obtaining execution approval.
